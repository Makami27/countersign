Your agent only pays signatures it has on file.

Countersign pays your regular payees in USDC on Sui. Like a bank comparing a cheque with the signature card, it checks every request against the wallet that signed for that payee, and refuses the rest.

The problem

Sending a payment to the wrong account on someone's say-so is one of the costliest frauds there is. In 2025 the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center counted $3.0 billion lost to business email compromise, where an email asks for a payment to go somewhere new.

Crypto has no bank to call back. In December 2025 one person sent about $50 million in USDT to a look-alike address copied from their own history.

An AI agent that pays your bills reads every message it gets, and it is never suspicious.

How it works

  1. The payee signs once

    When you add a payee, their own wallet signs a message proving the address is theirs. Until it does, the agent can't pay them.

  2. Messages can only suggest an address

    Emails, chats and invoices can name an address, but it is only ever kept as Stated, and stated addresses are never paid.

  3. A change needs two signatures, or you and a wait

    The wallet on file signs the change and the new wallet signs too. If the old wallet is lost, you approve it with your own wallet and it waits 24 hours, so you can still cancel.

Memory it can't be talked out of

Everything the agent relies on lives on Walrus Memory as records signed by your agent:

  • every payee address, and how it is known: proven by the payee's own wallet, or only said in a message
  • a receipt for every decision
  • your scheduled payments
  • your wallet

On every restart the agent rebuilds what it knows from Walrus Memory. If memory can't be read completely, nothing is paid.

Text shaped like a record but not signed by the agent is ignored, however official it looks.

What it does

  • Pays from Telegram or the app. Send "pay rent 0.10" to the bot, or press Pay in the owner app.
  • Scheduled payments every week, every two weeks, every month or every year.
  • Look-alike detection. An address that copies the start and end of a known one is refused.
  • A receipt for every decision, paid or refused, stored on Walrus Memory.
  • Approvals with your own wallet. Adding payees, loosening limits and confirming changes need its signature.
  • A lost-wallet path. You confirm the new address, it waits 24 hours, and you can cancel.
  • Telegram. Forward or paste any message about a payee to have it checked. /payees, /balance, /schedules, /cancel, /login and /help do what they say.
  • Several owners, each with their own agent, wallet and memory.

Built on Sui and Walrus, through Tatum

  • Every wallet signature is checked through Tatum's Sui gateway, including Slush accounts that sign in with Google (zkLogin).
  • USDC payments are sent through Tatum and looked up by their transaction id. A payment is never sent twice.
  • Memory lives on Walrus Memory mainnet.
  • Payments run on Sui testnet with test USDC unless a page says mainnet.

Questions

What if my landlord loses their old wallet?

You confirm the new address yourself in the owner app, signing with your wallet. Check it with your landlord first through contact details you already had, never ones from the message asking for the change. The change then waits 24 hours, during which you can cancel it, and the new wallet still has to sign. The address is checked against known scams and look-alikes when you confirm and again when it takes effect.

Can the AI be tricked into paying?

The model only reads messages and suggests what they mean. Code decides, and it pays only addresses whose owner's wallet signed; nothing the model returns can mark an address as verified. In our preregistered measurement, the same model and messages made an agent without these checks pay the attacker 71 times in 90. With Countersign it was 0.

Do I need a crypto wallet?

No, continue with Google. Signing in creates a free Sui wallet from your Google account (Slush). It signs approvals; it never pays. Already have a Sui wallet? Use that instead.

Which wallet do I fund?

There are two. Agent wallet: pays your bills (fund this one). Your wallet: signs approvals (never pays). Your own wallet only signs: it proves you approved a change, and a deposit is the one transfer it ever sends, when you press the button.

Who holds the money?

The agent wallet, which you fund. Its key is kept sealed on this server, so treat it like a hot wallet: keep small amounts in it. Caps limit what it can send per payment and per day.

What is stored on Walrus, and who can read it?

Payee addresses with how each was proven, receipts, schedules and your wallet, as records signed by your agent. The blobs on Walrus are encrypted, and this deployment reads them through its Walrus Memory account. Shared scam reports hold only an address, a reason and a receipt id.

What happens if Walrus, Tatum or the model is down?

Nothing is paid until the agent can check. If memory can't be read completely or Tatum can't give a definite answer, payments wait, and a payment whose outcome is unknown is never sent again. If the model is down, look-alikes and known scams are still refused by code, and plain pay commands still work.

Does it work on mainnet?

Memory already runs on Walrus Memory mainnet. Payments run on Sui testnet with test USDC for now, and every page says which network it uses.

What does it cost?

Trying this testnet deployment is free. Payments use test USDC, and Sui gas is paid in test SUI from the agent wallet.

Is it open source?

The code is public on GitHub, so you can read and check every rule. It isn't licensed for reuse.
